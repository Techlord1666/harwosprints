Large Format Printing in Lagos: What to Know Before Your Next Banner or Signage Order

Roll-up banners, billboards and shop signage — material choice, turnaround and the sizing mistakes that get orders reprinted.

Harwosprints·Updated July 2026·8 min read

A banner that looks crisp on your laptop screen can come out blurry, cropped wrong, or the wrong shade entirely once it's blown up to three metres wide. Large format printing punishes small mistakes because everything is magnified — literally. This guide covers what to get right before you place your next signage, banner or billboard order in Lagos.

What counts as "large format"

Anything too big for a standard desktop or office printer falls under large format — roll-up banners, event backdrops, billboards, shop fascia, window graphics and vehicle branding. These are produced on wide-format machines that print directly onto vinyl, film or rigid board rather than paper.

"At three metres wide, a design flaw you'd never notice on screen becomes the first thing everyone sees."

Send the right file — or your print pays for it

Vector files (AI, EPS, or a high-resolution PDF) scale cleanly to any size. A logo pulled from a website or a screenshot won't — it pixelates the moment it's stretched past its original resolution. If your only logo file is low-res, say so upfront so the design team can rebuild or clean it up before production, not after a wasted print run.

Sizing mistakes that get orders reprinted

  • Ignoring viewing distance — text sized to read on a phone screen is often unreadable on a billboard from the road. Bigger surfaces need bigger, simpler type.
  • Designing at low resolution — always design or supply artwork at, or scaled proportionally to, the final output size, not a thumbnail.
  • Forgetting bleed and safe zones — text or logos placed too close to the edge risk being trimmed off during finishing.
  • Wrong material for the environment — an indoor rigid board won't survive outdoor exposure, and outdoor vinyl is overkill (and pricier) for a one-day indoor event.

Turnaround to plan around

Standard production runs 3 to 5 working days once artwork is approved. Larger multi-piece jobs — full shop fascia, several billboard faces, or a large-scale event setup — may need extra lead time, so confirm your deadline when you order rather than assuming standard turnaround applies.

Large format work rewards planning. Send print-ready files, pick the material for where the piece will actually live, and build in review time for a job this size — the mistakes are only expensive because everything on a large format print is, by definition, hard to miss.

Frequently asked questions

What is large format printing used for?

Anything too big for a standard printer — roll-up banners, billboards, shop signage, event backdrops and vehicle branding — produced on wide-format machines onto vinyl, film or rigid board.

How long does large format printing take in Lagos?

Standard turnaround is 3 to 5 working days once artwork is approved. Larger or multi-piece orders may need extra lead time.

What file format do I need?

A vector file (AI, EPS, or high-resolution PDF) scales cleanly. Low-resolution images pixelate once enlarged — flag this early if it's all you have.

What's the difference between vinyl and film for signage?

Vinyl is thicker and more durable, suited to outdoor use. Film is thinner and more flexible, often used for window graphics or short-term promotions.
